# Cover letter structure — core rules

**Goal:** connect THIS person's specific real experience to THIS company's specific stated needs — never a generic letter that could apply anywhere.

## Writing-quality bar (applies even with no example letter to match)
A structurally correct letter can still read like generic AI marketing copy. Avoid the tells of that, regardless of whether a real example exists to follow:
- **No pain-point/hook clichés.** Never open with a construction like "At [Company], the hardest part of the job isn't X, it's Y" or "In a world where..." or any sentence built to sound clever before it says anything real. State the role and the person's relevant background plainly in sentence one.
- **No empty confidence-adjectives.** Don't describe your own fit as "a perfect match," "uniquely positioned," "deeply passionate," or similar — let the specific facts in the next clause do that work instead, or cut the adjective entirely.
- **Vary sentence length and avoid punchy-fragment rhythm.** A string of short, declarative, applause-line sentences ("I lead. I deliver. I execute.") reads as marketing copy, not as a real professional's letter. Prefer longer, information-dense sentences that subordinate detail naturally, the way someone describing their actual job would talk — a few clauses, specific nouns, not a slogan cadence.
- **Every sentence should contain a specific, checkable fact** — a number, a named institution/partner/system, a dated event, a concrete outcome — pulled from the real CV/profile. A sentence that could be deleted without losing any information (pure tone-setting or throat-clearing) should usually be cut.
- **Write like the applicant is narrating what they actually did**, not like a recruiter is summarizing why they're great. First-person, plain mechanics of the work (who was convinced, what was delivered, what broke and got fixed), not applause-line summaries of competencies.

- **Length and opening style are defaults only — if the user's own example letter(s) use a different word count, paragraph count, or opening style, follow the example instead; these generic defaults never override a real example.**
- **Length (default, no example to follow)**: 250-400 words, 3-4 paragraphs, professional business-letter format. Confident tone, not arrogant.
- **Opening (default, no example to follow)** (1-2 sentences): a real hook — specific company/role knowledge, a genuinely relevant past achievement, or a problem the role describes that the person has actually solved before. Avoid a bare "I saw your posting on LinkedIn." A direct, credential-forward opening like "I am writing to express my strong interest in..." is fine and often preferable to a rhetorical hook — match whatever the user's own examples do.
- **Body paragraph 1**: the single strongest real match between their experience and the role's top stated requirement, with a specific real result/metric already present in the CV.
- **Body paragraph 2**: additional real value, and — only if there's a genuine gap — address it briefly via a transferable skill or related real experience, never an apology.
- **Closing** (2-3 sentences): genuine enthusiasm for something specific about this role/company, a clear next step, thanks. Avoid passive filler like "I look forward to hearing from you."
- **No named hiring manager**: use "Dear Hiring Manager" or "Dear [Team] Team" — never "To Whom It May Concern."
- Every claim, metric, and story must come from the real CV/profile — never invented to fit the letter.
